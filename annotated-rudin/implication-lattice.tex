\title{\bfseries The Implication Lattice of Real Analysis\\[2pt]
  \large Limits, continuity, differentiability, integrability,
  and convergence}
\author{Shawn Zeng}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The relations among having a limit, continuity, differentiability, Riemann
integrability, and convergence are the substance of a first course in
analysis, but they are established piecemeal, each where it is needed, and
are nowhere assembled. We assemble them. For every ordered pair of these
properties we record either an implication, with proof, or a
non-implication, with an explicit function that witnesses it. Four of the
five properties are local and one is not, so the material divides into three
parts: the local implications at a point, the global ones on a compact
interval together with integrability, and the question of which properties
survive passage to a limit. Lebesgue's criterion makes the integrability
row exact rather than illustrative, and Volterra's example shows that the two
halves of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus are independent statements.
Seventeen implications and twelve counterexamples are recorded.
\end{abstract}
